# Fix the blockers, then prove the labels

TrueGaze's biggest risk in the four days before 22 Nov is not how the eyes look. Three things can sink it outright: the repo is still **private**, the copyrighted reference video `Eye Piece - Chris Jones (1080p, h264).mp4` is still tracked in a **1.5 GB `.git`**, and the README still says the demo link will be *"added at submission"* (all checked locally on 18 Nov). After those, the most valuable work is credibility, not looks. Two of the project's headline checks partly grade themselves. The launch-spec direction check in `wrapper/eyes.py` compares pupil positions the script computed itself, and those `pupil_center_2D` labels are an **unrefracted** projection of a point behind a cornea that Cycles refracts at IOR 1.376. The published synthetic-eye work this jury will know (EyeRender, RIT-Eyes, GazeGene) treats exactly that refracted-pupil label as the thing to get right. On looks, the field's best methods (Pixar's Samurai eye, CC Blender Tools, Universal Human) diagnose the team's remaining "painted iris / flat far-side sclera / crescent" faults as known Cycles problems with cheap fixes. But the team's own audit shows each look batch buying less (33 → 38 → 40 of 60 for about 27 render-hours), and that score comes from a single self-reviewer. The plan below therefore runs blockers (about 4 h), then an image-based label check and an honest motion-validation story (about 6 h), then one targeted look batch, if time remains, on the frames judges see first.

## No public precedent exists, so the two organizer rubrics are the whole target

Nothing public describes this hackathon, its judges or any past winner. Searches turn up only unrelated XR events, and the one public "Samsung Prism Hackathon" deck states no result ([SpeakerDeck](https://speakerdeck.com/metal0bird/samsung-prism-hackathon-team-pythonic)). PRISM itself is SRI-B's industry-academia R&D programme, built around mentored "worklets" and judged internally by patents filed ([Samsung Newsroom India](https://news.samsung.com/in/samsung-prism-new-industry-academia-program-in-india-to-collaborate-with-engineering-students-on-ai-iot-5g-launched)). The likely jury is SRI-B XR perception researchers who read a submission the way a reviewer reads a paper. That is an inference, not a documented fact.

The authoritative sources are therefore local. The 12 Nov Track 1 rubric weights **texture photorealism 60, kinematic accuracy 20, code and UV portability 20**, gives brownie points for more natural motion, and states that breaking guidelines means **direct disqualification** (`docs/hackathondetails.md`, `docs/organizer/Evaluation_for_SyntheticEyes.pptx`). The 5 Nov launch rubric was never withdrawn. It scored Direction as a 40-point pass/fail (all five of Left/Right/Up/Down/Reset), plus Calibration 20, Automation 20 and Sequence/metadata 20, and warned that **"exact folder layout matters; evaluators run code directly."** Whether both rubrics apply, and whether stage 1 is scored automatically, is unknown. Only prism@samsung.com can say.

Generic judging advice points the same way. Judges may spend about **five minutes per project**, so required items must be visible at the top and mapped to the published criteria ([DEV guide](https://dev.to/jacklynbiggin/how-to-make-your-devpost-submissions-not-suck-37i0)). Demo videos should lead with purpose, show the real thing working and respect the time limit ([HackHCC guide](https://hackhcc.gitbook.io/hackhcc-docs/other-resources/submission-and-judging-guide)). The 85 s silent, captioned demo already fits every stated limit. Its only problem is that nobody can reach it.

The local audit (18 Nov) finds a packaging gap on top of the visibility problem. The README's `zip -r … outputs/scene.blend wrapper outputs/launch_spec README.md` puts files under `outputs/…`, while the brief wants `scene.blend`, `wrapper/eyes.py`, `renders/`, `metadata.json` and `README.md` at the zip root. Worse, `wrapper/eyes.py` does `sys.path.insert(0, ROOT)` and `from truegaze import motion, scene`. An evaluator who unzips the bundle without `truegaze/` and "runs code directly" gets an ImportError. The brief also asks for **5–10 renders**, but `outputs/renders/` holds **33** PNGs and the README says 24. A five-minute judge cannot see the required set at a glance.

## The field validates synthetic eyes with labels and downstream use, not looks

Synthetic eye data splits into two families. Near-eye infrared renders serve headset tracking: Świrski & Dodgson's EyeRender in 2014, NVGaze, RIT-Eyes and LEyes. RGB eye-region and face renders serve remote gaze and landmarks: SynthesEyes, UnityEyes, Microsoft Face Synthetics and GazeGene. TrueGaze's closest ancestors are the two Blender pipelines. **EyeRender** modelled the eye and face in Blender with a Python module exposing pupil size, cornea refractive index, eye radius and lid position. It **ray-casts each pupil-border point through the cornea with a 1D root finder** to get the pupil outline the camera actually sees ([EyeRender](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/research/rainbow/projects/eyerender/)). **RIT-Eyes** (Blender 2.8, Cycles) uses a corneal radius of **7.8 mm at n = 1.3375**, lids that follow vertical gaze, 24 head models split **18 train / 6 test by identity**, and exports per-pixel pupil/iris/sclera masks plus 2D/3D feature centres and intrinsics ([RIT-Eyes](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2006.03642)). Both papers state the same consequence: a pupil seen through a refracting cornea is shifted and magnified, so the 2D label must be the refracted projection.

That bears directly on TrueGaze. `materials.py` sets `CORNEA_IOR = 1.376` and comments that Cycles refracts through the shell, so the iris "shifts with view angle". Meanwhile `wrapper/eyes.py:pose()` projects a point at `PUPIL_DEPTH` along the bone axis with `world_to_camera_view`, with no refraction. The labels are therefore systematically offset from the rendered pupil by an unknown amount. It is probably small at frontal views and grows at oblique gaze, but nobody has measured it. `direction_check()` then grades Left/Right/Up/Down on those same computed coordinates. A perception jury would call that circular, and the research notes and the team's own CLAUDE.md rule both name it an oracle advantage.

The canonical proof of usefulness in this field is **downstream performance on real held-out data**. NVGaze reached **2.06° ± 0.44°** on real subjects held out from training ([NVGaze](https://sites.google.com/nvidia.com/nvgaze)). RIT-Eyes reports segmentation mIoU dropping from about **95 same-domain to about 74** on real OpenEDS/NVGaze ([RIT-Eyes](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2006.03642)). Face Synthetics matched or beat real-trained landmarkers (300W NME **3.09 vs 3.37**), and its ablations showed augmentation and label adaptation mattered most ([Wood et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/html/2109.15102v2)). LEyes is the honesty check: plain Gaussian blobs beat photoreal-free baselines at pupil localisation (75.8 % within 2 px on OpenEDS2019), which shows that realism claims mean little unless they are tied to a task metric ([LEyes](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.06129v4)). Since 2025 the frontier has moved toward **full 3D eye-structure labels**. GazeGene, built from MetaHuman with geometry computed in Blender, ships per frame both visual and optic axes, a 3D gaze target, 2D/3D eyeball and pupil centres, 100-point iris contours and intrinsics, plus per-subject kappa and radii ([GazeGene HF card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/vigil1917/GazeGene)). GazePrior (May 2026) re-renders old device data for new headsets ([arXiv 2605.22359](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22359)).

Two conclusions follow for a four-day window. First, a **frozen-scorer check** is defensible and cheap. Detect the pupil in the 10 launch-spec PNGs with OpenCV, or with an off-the-shelf iris model such as MediaPipe, then report pixel error against `pupil_center_2D` and base the direction check on the detected pixels. It needs no training, it shares no generating function with the rig, and it uses the domain's own metric: pupil error in pixels, as in LEyes. Second, a sim-to-real gaze-training experiment cannot be done credibly by 22 Nov and should appear only as future work. A GazeGene-style per-frame label export is nearly free from a rig that already knows its eyeball centres and cornea. It strengthens the "dataset-ready" story for SRI-B's XR perception group, but it is a brownie item, not a rubric line.

Several numbers the research notes recalled from memory are **unverified** and must not appear in the deck: UnityEyes kNN about 9.9° and SimGAN about 7.8° on MPIIGaze. GazeGene's cross-dataset numbers were not retrieved either.

## Motion passes literature ranges, but those ranges were also the tuning targets

The kinematics are on solid ground. `tools/evaluate_motion.py` passes 15 of 15 checks against published ranges. Saccade duration is modelled as **25 ms + 2.4 ms/deg**, inside the published 20–30 ms + 2–2.7 ms/deg band. Baloh et al. measured a 2.7 ms/deg slope across 25 subjects ([Baloh 1975](https://scilit.com/publications/db6853d9de9d3c9a2d9c9b4fa59245a4)). Blinks follow the asymmetric shape the literature describes: closing in about **75–100 ms**, opening about half as fast, the whole blink **250–450 ms**, about **15/min** at rest ([Frontiers 2023 review](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/systems-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnsys.2023.1242654/full)). Trutoiu et al. showed with 32 and 43 participants that data-shaped asymmetric blinks are rated **more natural than textbook ease curves** ([Trutoiu 2011](https://la.disneyresearch.com/wp-content/uploads/Modeling-and-Animating-Eye-Blinks-Paper.pdf)), so this is a sourced claim worth one deck line.

The weakness is methodological, not numerical. `motion.py` picks "the middle of the published range" and `evaluate_motion.py` then checks the output against that same range. Passing therefore shows the code matches its specification. It does not show the eyes behave like real eyes. The eye-motion notes give the independent reference: **GazeBase**, with 12,334 recordings from 322 participants at 1,000 Hz on an EyeLink 1000, a random-saccade task, and a pymovements loader ([Griffith et al. 2021](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-021-00959-y.pdf); [pymovements](https://pymovements.readthedocs.io/en/v0.4.0/reference/pymovements.datasets.gazebase.GazeBase.html)). Fitting a main sequence on a handful of GazeBase subjects and overlaying TrueGaze's synthetic saccades would turn "inside a literature band" into "matches held-out human data". That is the same move the team's own hackathon rules demand. The GazeBase licence was not checked, so redistribute no derived data until it is. This is a real reword-or-rebuild fork. Rewording the README to "consistent with published ranges used to set the model" takes 10 minutes. The GazeBase overlay takes about 3–4 hours, with an unknown data-download cost.

The open physiology gaps line up with the brownie points for "more natural, human-like motion." The rig has **no pupil light response**: human constriction latency is roughly 200 ms, derived from a macaque 136 ms figure plus about 70 ms ([Utah record](https://collections.lib.utah.edu/details?id=225584)), and that derived value is flagged as an inference. The rig also lacks the **down-and-nasal eye rotation during a blink** ([Frontiers 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/systems-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnsys.2023.1242654/full)) and **1/f pink fixation noise**. Krejtz and Duchowski found that no jitter, or white-noise jitter, is rated least natural ([CAV 2017](https://zendy.io/title/10.1002/cav.1745)). Microsaccades at **1–3/s, under 0.5°** are well sourced ([Otero-Millan 2014](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/integrative-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnint.2014.00052/full)). Several numbers the notes could not source stay **unverified**: Baloh-type Vmax of about 500–700 °/s with C of about 7–15°, the Carpenter fit D ≈ 2.2A + 21 ms, the upper-lid gain against vertical gaze, gaze-evoked blink probability above 30–40°, the head-contribution threshold of about 15–20°, and the hippus band. Adding the blink rotation and pink-noise fixation is a motion-code change of about 2 hours, but it forces all three required videos to re-render.

## The remaining look faults are known Cycles artefacts with documented fixes

The self-audit's look faults map almost one-to-one onto failure modes that production eye builders document. Pixar's Samurai eye uses separate **cornea, limbus, lens, iris and meniscus** pieces. It builds the sclera like skin SSS at **IOR 1**, adds a warm **"limbus backlight"** that raises SSS distance to 8 around the iris, and excludes the meniscus from shadow casting to kill dark lines where it meets skin ([RenderMan: Making of a Samurai](https://renderman.pixar.com/the-making-of-a-samurai)). In Cycles, a refracting cornea **casts a shadow onto the iris**, and the standard fix is a cornea that does not cast shadows ([BlenderArtists](https://blenderartists.org/t/eye-glass-trancparancy-shadows-problem/1510333)). With refractive caustics on, the iris renders much darker, especially after denoising. Turning caustics off and raising iris albedo is the documented trade ([Universal Human docs](https://sites.google.com/view/universalhuman/documentation/eye-shader-docs)), and **Filter Glossy ≈ 1.0** cuts caustic fireflies ([Blender 4.5 Light Paths](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/4.5/render/cycles/render_settings/light_paths.html)). A "white ring" at the iris edge traces to sclera specular and to an unsealed limbus ([DAZ forum](https://www.daz3d.com/forums/discussion/comment/2949441/); [Reallusion](https://manual.reallusion.com/CC_and_IC_Auto_Setup_Plugin/ENU/CC_and_iC_Auto_Setup/1.2/02_for_Unreal/Eye-and-Eye-Occlusion.htm)).

The notes' diagnoses of TrueGaze's specific symptoms are **reasoned inferences, not sourced**, and need A/B renders to confirm. For the **hero-view flat far-side eye white**: turn off `visible_shadow` on the cornea, tear line and occlusion shells, give the sclera random-walk SSS with a near-neutral radius (about 1.0/0.85/0.75), and cap occlusion alpha at about 0.4–0.6. For the **iris crescent**: try refractive caustics off or Filter Glossy, seal the limbus with a dark ring, and use a larger key light. For the **pink outer eye white in Left/Right**: keep the eyeball a separate closed mesh so it never shares random-walk SSS with skin, use a neutral sclera radius instead of a skin-like red one, and desaturate corner vessels. For the **"painted, smeared" iris**: build it in polar coordinates with radial fibres as bump, a collarette at about 0.35–0.45 of the radius, Voronoi crypts near the collarette, a dark limbal ring and pupillary ruff, and a different noise seed per eye for distinct L/R irises. This layering follows Lefohn's ocularist approach and Universal Human's Base → Fibres → Melanin → Grain → Limbal stack ([Lefohn et al.](https://escholarship.org/content/qt8d88d3qc/qt8d88d3qc.pdf?t=ptt3cv); [Universal Human](https://sites.google.com/view/universalhuman/documentation/eye-shader-docs)). The ratios are artistic estimates, not measurements. For **canvas-noise skin**, a Voronoi-into-Bump pore layer distorted by noise, with a second cross-hatched micro-wrinkle bump and strength masked low on the lids, is the cheap standard. Skin bump looks more like leather than plain noise ([BlenderArtists](https://blenderartists.org/t/procedural-skin-texturing/1167816)). Every value in that recipe is a starting point to test, not a sourced preset.

The economics decide how much of this to do. Photorealism carries 60 points, but the team's own audit records **33 → 38 → 40 for about 27 h of rendering**. The next full batch (pink sclera plus iris on sets 05/07/08) is estimated at **3 h of work plus about 9.5 h of renders for 2–3 points**. Those are self-estimates from the same reviewer who made the changes, and no outside person has compared the renders to the reference video. The one look fix whose return is clearly above that curve is the **hero view**: it is the demo's opening frame, it costs only 1–2 h plus about 2.3 h of render, and the far-side-sclera and crescent fixes are the best documented of the set. Avoid the trap of a big "make it photoreal" batch that lands on 21 Nov, leaves no time to re-check portability, and puts the hard blockers at risk.

## Conclusion

The research changes the order of work more than its content. TrueGaze is already in the published Blender lineage of EyeRender and RIT-Eyes, and an Apache-2.0 procedural eye pipeline on an open head fills a real gap: the large comparable datasets are non-commercial. What separates it from that lineage is evidence, not rendering. A jury of XR perception researchers will trust a table that says "pupil labels match the rendered pupil within N px, measured by a detector that never saw our rig" far more than two extra look points. The same table exposes or retires the refraction offset that currently sits silently inside every `pupil_center_2D`. The cheapest high-impact moves are the ones that cannot be faked later: a public, clean, correctly zipped repo; independent image-based label and direction checks; and honest wording about which motion numbers were tuning targets versus held-out comparisons.

The team should also treat one decision as explicitly theirs rather than defaulting to the faster option: for both the direction check and the motion validation, **reword or rebuild**. Rebuilding the direction check is recommended because it is a 40-point pass/fail line and the current check is circular. Rebuilding motion validation against GazeBase is optional, and the deciding question is whether 3–4 hours there beat 3–4 hours on the hero shot.
